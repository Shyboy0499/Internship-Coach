# Internship Timeline

What to do when, from before day one to after the last day. This assumes a typical 10–12 week internship. For a longer co-op, stretch the middle; for a shorter placement, compress it and ask for feedback sooner.

## Contents

- Week one
- Weeks two to four

- Final weeks

## Before day one

- **Logistics.** Confirm start date and time, location or remote setup, who you report to, equipment, paperwork, and dress code. If anything is unclear, ask the recruiter.
- **Email your manager or mentor** with a short note and ask what to prepare (template in `templates.md`).
- **Learn the company and team:** product, customers, recent news, what your team owns.
- **Do focused skill prep** — see `role-skills.md`. Three to five things, not twenty.
- **Set two or three personal goals:** one skill to grow, one relationship goal (for example, meet everyone on the team), one thing to deliver.
- **Set up your notes doc and work log** so they're ready on day one.
- **Rest.** Arriving rested matters more than one more tutorial.

## Week one

- **Get access and setup working.** Flag access problems immediately — those are blockers you can't solve alone.
- **First meeting with your mentor.** Ask:
  - "What does a successful internship look like from your side?"
  - "How do you prefer I reach you with questions, and how often should we meet?"
  - "What's my project, and what's a good first milestone?"
  - "Who else should I meet?"
- **Write everything down:** names, roles, acronyms, commands, where things live.
- **Aim for a small first win:** a tiny fix, a docs correction, a quick analysis. It builds trust and teaches the workflow.
- **Introduce yourself** to the team and anyone you'll work with closely.

## Weeks two to four

- **Settle into an update rhythm** with your mentor (see the mentor playbook, section 5).
- **Put the project scope and milestones in writing** — a short plan your mentor approves.
- **Deliver something early and incrementally** rather than saving it all for a big reveal.
- **Have one or two coffee chats a week** with people outside your immediate work.
- **Ask for your first specific feedback** around week three.
- **Update your work log every Friday.**

## Midpoint

- **Write a self-review** (template in `templates.md`) and compare progress against the success criteria from week one.
- **Check yourself against the "meets vs. stands out" table** in `expectations.md` and pick one or two areas to push on in the second half.
- **Ask directly:** "Am I on track for a return offer? What would make it a clear yes?"
- **Adjust the plan** for the remaining weeks. Cut scope if needed — finishing a smaller thing well beats an unfinished big thing.

## Final weeks

- **Finish and polish** rather than starting new things.
- **Write a handoff doc:** what you did, where it lives, how to run it, what's left (template in `templates.md`).
- **Prepare the final presentation** and do a dry run with your mentor.
- **Have the return-offer conversation** if you haven't already.
- **Say thank you** to the people who helped, specifically. Connect on LinkedIn, and ask your mentor whether they'd be a reference.
- **Offboard properly:** return equipment and follow the company's checklist.

## After the internship

- **Update your resume** with concrete outcomes and numbers from your work log, without disclosing anything confidential.
- **Stay in touch.** A short, friendly note to your mentor a few months later keeps the relationship alive.
